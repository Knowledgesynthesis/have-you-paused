Have you PAUSED?

PAUSED

Introducing the Diagnostic Reasoning Vulnerability Appraisal (DRVA) — a bedside-to-M&M instrument that grades how vulnerable a diagnostic decision is to cognitive bias, without ever claiming a bias occurred.

Bashar Hasan, MD · Diagnostic Reasoning Vulnerability Project

Only the outcome differs. In Zwaan's experiment, physicians shown identical workups reported roughly twice as many biases when told the outcome was bad. Bias attribution is itself hindsight-biased — which is why "which bias was it?" is the wrong question to build a tool on.

Zwaan et al, BMJ Qual Saf 2017

The idea

Change the question.

Unanswerable

"Was this decision biased?"

Cannot be established in an individual case — and the attempt is contaminated by outcome knowledge.

→
Answerable

"Was it made under conditions where bias flourishes?"

An inherited label never re-derived. No named alternative. A misfit explained away. A plan running on momentum. All visible in the record — before the outcome is known.

The DRVA grades those conditions — reasoning vulnerability — through domains, signaling questions, and graded judgments. Never a verdict that bias occurred.

The data never changed. The label did. DRVA Domain 1 asks exactly this: would the same data, arriving with a different label, plausibly have led elsewhere?

The tool

Eight domains. Tap any of them.

By design

Judgments, not arithmetic.

Low concernSome concernHigh concernNot enough information

No total score

Domains are not exchangeable — a High in disconfirming evidence cannot be offset by Lows elsewhere. Explicit rules, not arithmetic, govern the overall judgment.

Every judgment meets its remedy

Each domain's corrective action is printed beside its judgment — flag a concern, see the fix. High concern ends in a diagnostic pause with fresh eyes.

The decision, not the clinician

"High concern" means the conditions favored bias — which happens to every clinician on every busy day. Nothing feeds a performance review.

Will it survive a real shift?

The pause fits. It just needs content.

In a 2026 four-hospital pediatric pilot, a team diagnostic time-out ran at unplanned PICU transfers:

>90%

of time-outs took 5 minutes or less — the pause is feasible inside real pediatric workflows.

28%

agreed it actually changed or added to the differential — the lowest-rated value item. An unstructured pause doesn't generate diagnostic content.

That gap is precisely what DRVA-8 supplies: eight questions that give the time-out something to find.

A shared M&M vocabulary

Case review examines the reasoning, not the resident.

Psychological safety by construction

Uncertainty documentation and specific safety-netting become reviewable.

The paper

Synthesis, not invention — and the gap is real.

The gap, verified

A logged PubMed search (ten queries, no date limits, 926 records), and every existing tool reviewed one by one:

  • Checklists prompt but don't grade
  • Safer Dx detects error, not vulnerability
  • Bias taxonomies can't reach the bedside
  • No instrument grades decision-level reasoning vulnerability

Every ingredient is inherited

Domain-and-signaling-question architecture from RoB 2. Checklist practicality from Ely. Pocket brevity from TWED. The time-out from Trowbridge. Hindsight defenses from incident analysis.

The combination is new.

Optional future work

If the tool earns interest, each claim gets its test.

STEP 1

Expert panel review

Do the questions measure the right thing?

A multispecialty panel rates every question; wording is revised until the panel agrees.

STEP 2

Think-aloud interviews

Do users read it as intended?

Clinicians talk through the tool while completing it; any question that gets misread is rewritten.

STEP 3

Usability pilot

Does it fit the workflow?

Timed, real-world use on a ward, in an emergency department, and at an M&M conference.

STEP 4

Reliability testing

Do two raters agree?

Two clinicians judge the same cases independently; their judgments are compared.

STEP 5

Hindsight experiment

Does the design resist hindsight?

Reviewers rate identical cases, half knowing the outcome, half not. DRVA judgments should shift less than bias-labeling does.

The steps run in this order, each gating the next — and all of it is deliberately optional, to be taken up if the tool finds users and collaborators. No claim of benefit is made before these tests are done.
